When you take your child out in the pram on a hot day, what do you usually worry about?

Lisa

Mainly that they’re comfortable and not getting too hot, especially if they’ve fallen asleep. When they’re asleep you don’t really want to keep disturbing them just to check if they’re okay.

How do you currently tell whether your child is too hot or uncomfortable?

Lisa

Usually I’ll feel their neck or their back, or just look at whether they’re red or sweaty. But it’s definitely not very exact. A lot of the time you’re just going off instinct.

Are there times when it’s difficult to know how hot it actually is inside the pram?

Lisa

Yeah at times, especially because you’re standing above the pram and they’re lower down underneath the canopy. You can feel comfortable yourself but that doesn’t necessarily mean they do. I think that’s probably the hardest part, you can’t actually feel what they’re experiencing.

Have you ever changed what you were doing because you were worried your child was getting too hot?

Lisa

Definitely. I’ve cut walks short before or gone into a café or shop just to get into air conditioning. If it’s really hot I’ll also try and plan around it and go out earlier in the morning instead.

Would it be useful to know the temperature inside the pram rather than just the outside temperature?

Lisa

Yeah, I think it would give you a lot more peace of mind. Especially if they’re sleeping, you wouldn’t have to keep checking them every few minutes. You could actually know if the temperature was getting too high.

How would you want a system like ThermoTots to warn you?

Lisa

Probably just a notification if it actually became a problem. I wouldn’t need to know the temperature every second. Something saying it’s getting too hot and you should cool them down would be useful.

What would make you trust or distrust the technology?

Lisa

I’d want to know it had been properly tested, especially because it involves babies. I think parents would need to know that they can rely on it and that the warning level is based on something meaningful rather than just an arbitrary temperature.

Is there anything that would stop you from using something like this?

Lisa

If it made things more complicated, probably. Parents already have enough things to remember. I’d want to be able to attach it to the pram and basically forget about it until it actually needed to tell me something.

Can you tell me about a time you felt dangerously hot, dehydrated or unwell at a festival?

Gabe

Yeah, there was one festival where it was just absolutely boiling and I’d been in the crowd for ages. I started feeling pretty cooked, like really light-headed and just drained. I tried to ignore it for a bit because I didn’t want to leave, but eventually I was like, nah, I need to get out of here. I ended up going to the medic tent and just sitting there for a while, drinking water and cooling down.

What did you notice first when you started feeling overheated?

Gabe

Probably just being insanely thirsty at first and feeling way more tired than normal. Then I started getting a bit dizzy and that was when I realised it was probably more than just being hot. It kind of creeps up on you though, so you don’t really notice straight away.

How do you currently decide when you need water, shade or a break?

Gabe

Honestly, I just go off how I’m feeling. If I’m really thirsty or starting to feel wrecked, I’ll go get water or sit down somewhere. There’s not really much thought behind it, it’s normally just when I feel like I actually need it.

Are there situations where you might ignore signs that you are overheating? Why?

Gabe

Yeah, definitely. If you’re watching someone you really want to see or you’ve got a good spot near the front, you don’t really want to leave. Especially if all your mates are staying there. You kind of just tell yourself you’ll be fine and you can push through it for a bit longer.

How easy is it to find water, shade or medical help when you need it?

Gabe

Usually it’s there, but actually getting to it can be a pain. If you’re right in the middle of a packed crowd, getting out can take ages. And sometimes you don’t really know exactly where the water or medics are unless you’ve already seen them earlier.

Have you ever seen a friend become unwell from heat, dehydration or drug use? What happened?

Gabe

Yeah, a few times. Usually someone starts looking really pale or they’re saying they feel dizzy or sick, and then everyone’s like, alright, we need to get you some water or get you out of the crowd. Most of the time you only really notice once they’re already feeling pretty bad.

How would you feel about technology placed around the festival that could detect when parts of the crowd were becoming dangerously hot?

Gabe

I reckon that would be pretty useful, especially near the front where it gets really packed. When I got sick, I didn’t really realise how bad it was until I was already feeling dizzy. If staff could see that an area was getting too hot before people started getting sick, that would probably help a lot.

What would make you trust or distrust a system like that?

Gabe

I’d trust it if you could actually see that something happened because of it. Like if staff brought more water over, opened up space or medics came closer when an area was getting bad. If it was just measuring the temperature and nothing changed, then I don’t really see the point.

Is there anything that would make you uncomfortable about that kind of technology?

Gabe

Not really if it was just monitoring the heat or the crowd as a whole. I’d probably be a bit weirded out if it was tracking individual people or using facial recognition or something. But if it’s basically just there to tell staff that one part of the crowd is getting unsafe, I wouldn’t really have an issue with it.

There the parents worry the room is too cold instead.

Lovely, then what problems or unmet needs do you currently see around keeping babies comfortable or safe in hot conditions?

Dan

The main problem is that parents doesnt really have a way to know how hot the baby really is. They just put a hand on the back of the neck. I think a few things they miss: is how hot a car seat gets after an hour parked in Bangkok, strollers sitting low down where the road is hottest, and carrying the baby in a sling at an outdoor market.

What would you say makes a feature commercially attractive in this market?

Dan

Price bracket comes first. The market is split between premium at the top and very cheap at the bottom, with not much in between. So we ask whether the feature pushes the product into a bracket where we can still win. Next, can we show it in thirty seconds? Can you describe how customers in Thailand respond to baby products that include monitoring, sensing, alerts, or connected technology?

Dan

It goes both ways. Anything that calms a worried parent sells well, like cameras or anything that sounds like it prevents something bad. First-time parents especially. Connected products are harder.
